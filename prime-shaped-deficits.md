# Prime-Shaped Deficits: A Numerical Anatomy of Weil Positivity

*Numerical experiments on the Weil quadratic form, the relay structure of positivity, and the uniqueness of the prime tone system. August 2026.*

## Abstract

Weil's criterion states that the Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to the positivity of an explicit quadratic form Q built from the primes, the pole of the zeta function, and an archimedean term. We implement Q as a matrix over a family of Gaussian test functions, validate it against the first 500 nontrivial zeros to 7 significant digits, and measure five structural properties: (1) the prime "tone system" {k·ln p, weight ln p·p^(−k/2)} is a razor-sharp critical point of positivity — shifting any length, scaling any weight in either direction, or injecting any tone at a composite location breaks positivity linearly in the perturbation; (2) the positivity margin decays exponentially with test-function support length at measured rate ≈ 22.2 ≈ πγ₁/2, the Slepian–prolate concentration constant of the first zero; (3) positivity is sustained by a relay: the archimedean part alone fails at ~ln 2, and each prime restores positivity exactly until the next prime's logarithm; (4) the deficit of each partial form is pinned to the logarithms of the missing primes, not to the geometry of the test window — the failure mode of the continuum is a portrait of the remaining primes; (5) in a pilot uniqueness test, all density-matched Cramér-random competitors and Beurling dilations fail positivity by up to six orders of magnitude relative to the true primes. We relate these observations to the Connes–Consani program and state a classification conjecture. *Sections 9–11, added after a second universality test and an instrument audit, correct property (2): the measured decay rate is a property of the Gaussian test family as much as of the arithmetic; see §10.*

## 1. Setup

For a real test function f supported (effectively) in [0, L], let g(x) = ∫ f(t) f(t+x) dt and h(r) = |f̂(r)|². The Weil quadratic form is

Q(f) = h(i/2) + h(−i/2) − g(0)·ln π + (1/2π) ∫ h(r)·Re ψ(1/4 + ir/2) dr − Σ_{n≥2} Λ(n) n^(−1/2) [g(ln n) + g(−ln n)],

where Λ is the von Mangoldt function and ψ the digamma function. Weil's criterion: RH ⟺ Q(f) ≥ 0 for all f. By the explicit formula, Q(f) = Σ_ρ h(γ_ρ) over the nontrivial zeros — a sum of squares if and only if all zeros lie on the critical line.

We work over the basis f_k(x) = exp(−(x−μ_k)²/2σ²), μ_k equally spaced, reducing Q to a symmetric matrix M; positivity on the band is λ_min(M) ≥ 0 (computed as a generalized eigenvalue relative to the basis overlap matrix). The matrix built from primes + Γ-factor agrees with the matrix built independently from the first 500 zeros to max entrywise error 7×10⁻⁷, confirming the implementation.

A preliminary observation (Landau's theorem, verified on our zeros): the superposition S(u) = Σ_j cos(γ_j u) over the first 500 zeros shows dips at u = ln n of predicted depth −(T/2π)Λ(n)/√n. Measured vs predicted at n = 2,...,13 agree within ~2%, including the required *silences* at the composites n = 6 (observed +1.4, predicted 0) and n = 10 (+1.1, predicted 0). The dips are negative — the zeros are absorption lines, not emission lines.

## 2. Criticality of the prime tone system

With σ = 0.20 and nine basis functions, λ_min for the true primes is 0 to numerical precision (+1.1×10⁻¹⁹ on the exact zero-side form). Perturbations:

| Perturbation | Result |
|---|---|
| Shift ℓ₂ = ln 2 → ln 2 + ε (harmonics coherent) | λ_min ≈ −0.85·\|ε\| — V-shaped, fails both directions |
| Scale weight w₂ → (1+δ)w₂ | fails both directions (−0.31δ below, −1.0δ above) |
| Inject tone at ln 6, weight w | λ_min ≈ −0.65·w — any amplitude at a composite breaks positivity |

The two-sided failure of the weight perturbation is notable: because autocorrelations may be negative at nonzero lags, under-weighting a prime is punished as surely as over-weighting. The prime system is a genuine critical point, not merely a cone boundary. The V-shaped (non-smooth, |ε|-linear) vertex is the signature of zero slack, consistent with the Rodgers–Tao theorem that the de Bruijn–Newman constant satisfies Λ ≥ 0: RH, if true, is only barely true.

## 3. Gap decay: the prolate rate

Using 45-digit arithmetic, the positivity margin (smallest generalized eigenvalue of the exact zero-side form) decays with support length L as

gap ~ exp(−22.2·L),  measured over 15 orders of magnitude (10⁻¹⁴ → 10⁻²⁹, L = 0.9 → 2.4),

against the reference constant πγ₁/2 = 22.2013 (γ₁ = 14.1347 the first zero). Mechanism, visible in the minimizer: its spectral mass always hides in the zero-free window [0, γ₁), and the leakage of a support-L function out of a band of width γ₁ is the Slepian–prolate concentration rate e^(−πγ₁L/2). The optimizer, given no theory, independently converges on the prolate strategy that Connes–Consani adopted by design (Weil positivity and trace formula, Selecta Math. 27 (2021)). The decay proceeds in quantized steps — steep drops alternating with plateaus as each new degree of freedom cancels one more spectral constraint.

This curve quantifies why RH resists computation: the margin to be certified vanishes exponentially in the band while any fixed-precision method carries constant noise. Only an argument with exact structural content survives the limit.

**[Correction — see §10.]** The rate reported above was measured over a Gaussian test family, whose Fourier tails decay super-exponentially — faster than Paley–Wiener permits for genuinely compactly supported functions. The clean numerical match to πγ₁/2 may therefore be partly fortuitous; the compact-support (DPSS) measurement in §10 is the physically honest quantity, and the full decay law awaits re-measurement in that frame. The qualitative conclusions (exponential vanishing of the margin; hide-below-γ₁ strategy; staircase structure) stand.

## 4. The relay

Cumulative-prime positivity windows (σ = 0.07; nominal thresholds smeared by ~0.2–0.3 from Gaussian tails):

| Form | positive until ≈ | first clear failure |
|---|---|---|
| archimedean only (pole + Γ, no primes) | ~0.5 | −0.21 at L = 0.69 |
| + prime 2 | ~0.85 | −5.8×10⁻⁴ at L = 0.95 |
| + primes 2, 3 | ~1.3 | −6.2×10⁻³ at L = 1.40 (≈ ln 4? no — see below) |
| + primes 2, 3, 5 | ~1.65 | −4.2×10⁻² at L = 1.75 |
| + primes 2, 3, 5, 7 | ~2.0 | −1.7×10⁻³ at L = 2.10 |
| all primes | ≈ 0 (noise floor) at every L | never |

Note ln 4 requires no rescue: it is 2², already covered by prime 2's harmonics. Each failure aligns (after smear correction) with the *next* prime's logarithm: ln 3 = 1.099, ln 5 = 1.609, ln 7 = 1.946, ln 11 = 2.398. This reproduces, in an independent implementation, the finding of Connes–Consani (Spectral triples and ζ-cycles, arXiv:2106.01715) that archimedean positivity extends slightly beyond log 2 and is restored on [log 2, log 3) precisely by the prime 2.

## 5. The rescue mechanism: deficits are prime-shaped

How does a term entering Q with a minus sign rescue positivity? The minimizer of the {archimedean + prime 2} form at L = 1.10 has autocorrelation g(ln 3) = −0.376, while g(ln 5) = g(ln 6) = 0.0000: the deficit is localized exactly at the next prime and nowhere else. Prime 3's term −w₃[g(ln 3)+g(−ln 3)] = +0.477 covers the deficit λ_min = −0.385.

The decisive test: hold the prime set at {2} and grow the window past ln 3. The dip does *not* track the window edge — it stays pinned near ln 3 (locations 0.99–1.16 for L = 1.10 → 2.00), and as further primes come within reach the deficit distributes onto them (g(ln 5) = −0.38, g(ln 7) = −0.19 at L = 2.0). Once prime 3 is included, g(ln 3) flips positive (+0.23) and the dip relocates to ln 5. The failure of each partial form is a portrait of the missing primes.

This is exactly what the explicit-formula identity predicts: the partial form equals (zero-side sum) + (remaining primes' terms), so the minimizer profits precisely from the terms not yet included, and each arriving prime cancels its own shadow — exactly, automatically. Timing and amount of every rescue are theorems. What is *not* automatic — the entire content of RH — is that the zero-side sum is a sum of squares: that the primes are the only debtors, with no off-line zero hiding as a deficit shaped like nothing, at a location that is no ln p^k, which no tone exists to pay. Through L = 2.4 every deficit we observe is prime-shaped.

## 6. Uniqueness pilot: no competitor balances the books

Is the prime system merely locally critical, or unique among density-matched competitors? Pilot (L = 2.0): Cramér-random pseudo-prime systems (each integer n kept with probability 1/ln n, same tone/weight rule) versus the true primes:

| System | λ_min |
|---|---|
| true primes | −5.8×10⁻⁷ (≈ 0, noise floor) |
| Cramér draws (6 seeds) | −0.80 to −1.24 |
| Beurling dilation, lengths (1+d)·ln p, d = 0.001 | −2.2×10⁻³ |
| d = 0.005 / 0.02 | −1.1×10⁻² / −4.3×10⁻² |

All competitors fail — the random ones by six orders of magnitude. Matching prime *statistics* is worthless; only the actual multiplicative atoms of ℤ balance the fixed archimedean continuum.

**Classification conjecture (restatement of RH + uniqueness, in the spirit of Dyson's 2009 quasicrystal proposal).** Among countable tone systems {(ℓ_i, w_i)} in ℝ₊, the system {(k ln p, ln p·p^(−k/2))} is the unique one for which the functional Q remains nonnegative on test functions of every support length. The non-resonance of the lengths (ℤ-linear independence, equivalent to unique factorization) is necessary; the conjecture is that the archimedean place then pins the system entirely.

## 7. Relation to current work and open problems

The base case of the relay is a theorem: Connes–Consani prove archimedean positivity as the trace of the scaling action compressed onto the complement of phase-space cutoff projections, controlled by prolate spheroidal functions (Selecta Math. 27, 2021). Each relay pass is finitely checkable; the equivalence of RH with a sequence of finite properties P(n) involving only Euler factors below n is established in Connes–Consani–Moscovici (Zeta zeros and prolate wave operators, Ann. Funct. Anal. 15 (2024)). The missing ingredient is the conservation law that guarantees every pass — the analogue of Riemann–Roch on the Jacobian in Weil's function-field proof. The object intended to carry that law is under construction: Connes–Consani, "On the Jacobian of Spec Z" (J. Noncommutative Geometry, to appear; preprint July 2026) interprets the Riemann sector of the adele class space as a monoidal extension of the Picard group of the compactified Spec Z, with elements given by rank-1 torsion-free abelian groups carrying norm data extending Arakelov metrized line bundles.

Open experimental directions on this rig: (a) sharpen the uniqueness pilot into a systematic classification over structured competitor families (Beurling systems with prescribed zeta-pole behavior); (b) measure the relay for Dirichlet L-functions, where Siegel-zero phenomena would appear as anomalous rescue schedules; (c) track the per-pass surplus (rescue minus deficit) as a function of prime index, the empirical shadow of the conjectural Riemann–Roch inequality.

## 8. Addendum: universality test on L(s, χ₄)

To test whether the anatomy is a law of L-functions rather than a property of ζ, we repeated the program for the Dirichlet beta function (odd character mod 4: no pole, conductor 4, prime 2 invisible, primes ≡ 3 mod 4 entering with flipped sign). We computed its first 18 zeros from scratch via Hurwitz zeta (first zero 6.0209) and validated the beta explicit-formula matrix against them to 2.7×10⁻⁶. Four predictions were pre-registered.

P1 (confirmed): with no pole, the tone-free form is funded by the conductor term ln(4/π) and the Γ-factor, and stays positive until ≈ ln 3 — twice zeta's window — exactly until the function's own first prime. The correct general slogan is not "the pole pays for the primes" but "the local data at the archimedean place pays for the finite places."

P2 (core confirmed, refinement failed): the deficit at ln 3 is a positive bump (+0.31) where zeta's was a dip (−0.38), matching χ(3) = −1: deficits are signed by the character. The naive per-tone signed portrait failed at ln 5 (+0.04, predicted negative): autocorrelation positive-definiteness couples nearby lags.

P3 (confirmed): the relay runs on beta's own schedule — {3} fails near ln 5, {3,5} near ln 7, {3,5,7} near ln 11, with 9 = 3² auto-covered and 2 absent.

P4 (failed as stated): the gap decay is not globally πγ₁/2 ≈ 9.46; measured ≈ −29 over L ∈ [0.9, 2.4] with strong staircase structure. Extended runs show local slopes of −9.05 and −9.63 on L ∈ [2.4, 2.8] — bracketing the prolate prediction — before basis saturation (minimizer degenerating to the lowest-frequency mode) flattened the curve artificially. Revised hypothesis, unresolved on this rig: a two-regime law — an early regime governed by zero density (beta's zeros are ~2× denser at low height by the conductor effect, its zero-free hideout [0, 6.02) ~2× narrower), transitioning to the prolate rate πγ₁/2 once the support can resolve the window. Resolving the asymptote requires a prolate-adapted basis.

Scoreboard: the relay anatomy, character-signed deficits, and archimedean funding generalized cleanly; the quantitative decay law did not. **[The "two-regime" hypothesis and basis-saturation diagnosis above are superseded by the instrument audit in §10.]**

## 9. Second universality test: L(s, χ₃)

A third L-function (odd quadratic character mod 3; γ₁ predicted ≈ 8.04) was tested with all predictions pre-registered and zero adjustable parameters, including one prediction unique to this character: since χ₃(3) = 0, the relay must skip 3 entirely.

Zeros were computed from scratch via Hurwitz zeta: first zero 8.0397. Explicit-formula validation: 2.8×10⁻⁶. Results: the tone-free window dies at ≈ ln 2 (like ζ, unlike β — each function's continuum is calibrated to its own first prime, here funded despite a *negative* conductor term ln(3/π) < 0); the deficit at ln 2 is a positive bump (+0.34, matching χ₃(2) = −1); and the relay skips 3 exactly — the {2}-form passes straight through ln 3 (+5.9×10⁻⁵ at L = 1.30) and fails only approaching ln 5, then {2,5} → ln 7, {2,5,7} → ln 11. The Gaussian-frame decay transition was measured at L ∈ [1.65, 1.80] against the pre-registered 1.75 (calibrated on β with no freedom), with post-transition slopes −12 to −16 against the predicted πγ₁/2 = 12.6. Across β and χ₃ the transition satisfies γ₁·L* ≈ 14; per §10 this invariant is a property of the Gaussian frame pending re-derivation.

Cumulative universality scoreboard across ζ, β, χ₃: relay anatomy 3/3, character-signed deficits 3/3, archimedean-funds-the-primes 3/3, character-dictated relay schedule (including the skip) 3/3.

## 10. Instrument audit: compact support vs Gaussian frames (correction)

Replacing the Gaussian basis with discrete prolate spheroidal sequences (DPSS — strictly supported, band-concentrated, the optimal Slepian family) exposed a systematic error in the decay-law measurements. At L = 2.4 for β, the DPSS gap is ln(gap) = −25.9, consistent with the pure prolate law −πγ₁L/2 = −22.7 up to polynomial prefactors — while the Gaussian frame reported −42.3, an impossibly small value for compactly supported functions. The resolution: Gaussians are not compactly supported, and their super-exponential Fourier decay e^(−σ²γ²) violates the Paley–Wiener limit that binds genuine support-L functions. The Gaussian decay curves therefore conflate the arithmetic positivity structure with the test family's own spectral privilege.

Consequences: (i) the two-regime law of §8 and the γ₁·L* ≈ 14 invariant of §9 are Gaussian-frame phenomena, pending re-derivation; (ii) part of the ζ rate's match to 22.20 in §3 may be fortuitous; (iii) at the single compact-support point measured, the simple prolate law holds directly, with no second regime needed. Required follow-up: re-measure all decay curves in the DPSS frame at ~60-digit precision (the DPSS Gram matrices are provably PSD, so any negative eigenvalue is an arithmetic-precision failure — observed at L ≥ 3.6 at 35 digits). All relay, deficit-pinning, uniqueness, and surplus results are unaffected: they are O(1) measurements far above any frame systematics.

Three findings. First, for ordinary L-functions in the separable regime the per-pass surplus is small, positive, and remarkably uniform (+0.04 to +0.10) across all three systems — the relay runs everywhere at near-exact change. Second, ζ's pass p = 11 shows a negative frozen-minimizer surplus: not a positivity violation (the full form remains ≥ 0 after re-optimization) but a *separability breakdown* — ln 13 − ln 11 = 0.167 is below the instrument resolution (~0.3), so the p = 11 deficit already contains 13's shadow, and rescues become collective. Auditing pass p individually requires resolution ~ the local gap in {ln p}, which shrinks like 1/p: the bookkeeping cost of the induction grows without bound — a fourth measured face of the difficulty.

Third, the exceptional character. χ₋₁₆₃ (discriminant −163, the class-number-one character, all primes p ≤ 37 inert with χ(p) = −1) runs a categorically different economy: instead of zero margin, the huge conductor income ln(163/π) ≈ 3.95 banks a buffer of ≈ +3.9, and the inert primes *drain* it — rescues near zero or negative, surplus falling monotonically 4.07 → 2.45 over six passes. Exceptional characters do not balance; they draw down. GRH for such characters is the statement that the drawdown never overdraws — the Siegel-zero problem, photographed in the ledger. Proposed measurement program: map buffer-minus-drawdown across a family of quadratic conductors against L(1, χ); a clean law there would be the empirical shadow of the missing positivity inequality exactly where exceptional-zero physics lives.

## 12. Extending the ledger: the conductor family and the parity burn law

Four further L-functions were added: χ₅ (even real), χ₇ (odd real), χ₈ (even real, 2 invisible), and the primitive order-3 character mod 9 (even, complex — our first, requiring root-number rotation to locate its asymmetric zeros; first positive zero 5.32). Both new structural machines validated against independently computed zeros on first attempt (3.2×10⁻⁶ and 2.4×10⁻⁶). χ₈'s relay skips 2 exactly as its character demands, extending the skip demonstrations to a second instance.

The headline is a correction with a discovery inside it. The "eerie uniformity" of §11 does not survive the larger family: surpluses now range from +0.0013 to +0.68, and early passes at larger conductors show *negative deficits* — the form is still in surplus when the first primes arrive, a buffer phase absent at q = 3, 4. The structure that replaces uniformity is quantitative. Measured first-pass buffers against conductor income ln(q/π): χ₇ 0.78 vs 0.80; χ₋₁₆₃ 3.93 vs 3.95 (odd characters: gap ≈ 0.02); χ₅ 0.018 vs 0.465; χ₉ 0.61 vs 1.05 (even characters: gap ≈ 0.445). Empirical law: starting buffer ≈ ln(q/π) − c_parity, with c_odd ≈ 0.02 and c_even ≈ 0.445 — a parity-dependent archimedean burn rate, consistent with the gap between ψ(3/4) and ψ(1/4) in the respective Γ-kernels, matched by two characters per parity to ±0.005.

Two consequences. First, the χ₋₁₆₃ "anomaly" of §11 is demoted and thereby explained: it is not a different economy but the far end of a smooth conductor-indexed family running from zero-margin (small q) through buffer-and-relay (q = 7, 8, 9) to buffer-and-drawdown (q = 163). Exceptionality is a position on a measured curve, not a separate phenomenon. Second, the complex character scales the entire ledger: with tone strengths Re χ(p) = −½ (and a full-strength +1 tone at 8 = 2³, since ω³ = 1), deficits and rescues shrink proportionally, and late passes run at razor margins (deficit 0.0062, rescue 0.0097 at p = 11) while remaining positive — tone strength sets the economy's scale, positivity holds at every scale.

A cultural footnote: this section began as a test of "3-6-9" numerology. The rigorous content of mod-9 arithmetic is: 6 is not a valid conductor (no primitive character mod 6 exists), and the primitive mod-9 characters are blind to 3 and 9 (χ = 0 there). The rigorous magnificence of 3, 6, 9 at their own modulus is invisibility.

A physical negative control closes the section. The hydrogen atom is the one physical system with an exact zeta identity: levels ∝ 1/n² with degeneracy n² make its spectral zeta function analytically continue to ζ(−2s−2) — the Riemann zeta function in disguise. Yet its spectrum carries none of zeta's music: the Rydberg line set (153 distinct lines, n < m ≤ 18) has local-unfolded spacing variance 0.898 — Poisson-clustered, integrable-class — against the zeros' rigid 0.153 (GUE ≈ 0.18); and as a tone system in the zeta ledger, hydrogen fails at λ_min ≈ −3.1 in both budget-matched variants, the worst failure measured on this rig (3× worse than the Cramér random ensemble, whose tones at least sat at logarithms of integers). The distinction this measures: physics is saturated with zeta's *values* (spectral zeta identities, ζ(2), ζ(3) in QED couplings), but the *zeros* belong to the opposite dynamical class — any physical Hilbert–Pólya system is hereby measured to be non-integrable: chaotic, level-repelling, hydrogen's dynamical opposite.

## 14. The theorem case: the Selberg ledger of the modular surface

Queue item (g) executed. Using the exact trace formula for PSL(2,ℤ)\ℍ (Booker–Platt, arXiv:1710.00603, Prop. 2.1), the quadratic form Q = I + E + P + D − h(i/2) — identity, elliptic, parabolic, discrete, minus the constant eigenfunction — was implemented with geodesic multiplicities computed from scratch as class numbers of indefinite binary quadratic forms (h(5)=1, h(12)=2, h(21)=2; the trace-7 length landing at exactly 2× the trace-3 length confirmed the power structure). Two memory-based reconstruction attempts failed validation (residuals 3.7×10⁻² and 1.6×10⁻²) before fetching the exact formula; the fetched version validated against the known Maass eigenvalues at 4.8×10⁻⁴ on spectral scale 2.8×10⁻² (~1.7%), after correcting an integration-grid truncation the theorem itself exposed (the guaranteed-positive full form read negative). The proof's 1/π normalization of the Λ(n)/n sum beat the proposition display's by a factor of 200.

Structure of this ledger, sign-mirrored relative to ζ: the pole (constant eigenfunction) is an *expense*, the tones are *income*, and the discrete side interleaves two currencies — closed geodesics at lengths ℓ (weights ℓ₀/2sinh(kℓ₀/2), the same half-density law as primes) *and arithmetic tones at 2·ln n with weights Λ(n)/n* entering through the cusp, with the scattering matrix built from ξ(2s−1)/ξ(2s). The Riemann zeta function is literally inside this surface's archimedean books.

Measured relay (σ=0.07): tone-free form fails approaching 2ln2=1.386; adding the 2ln2 arithmetic tone holds positivity to the first geodesic 1.9248; adding the geodesic holds to 2ln3=2.197; adding 2ln3 holds through the trace-4 geodesic window; the full form sits at the numerical noise floor (−(2–7)×10⁻³), consistent with theorem-guaranteed positivity. The relay baton alternates between primes and geodesics. Pinning confirmed with mirrored sign: the tone-free minimizer at L=1.7 shows a *positive bump* g(2ln2)=+0.394 (exceeding the nearer control g(1.0)=+0.303) — hunger for income where ζ's ledger showed dips of debt.

Conclusion for the queue question: the ledger anatomy (relay, pinning, half-density weights) is a property of trace formulas in general, not of arithmetic alone — but in the one physics case where the Riemann hypothesis is a theorem, the anatomy is *jointly carried* by geometry and arithmetic, entangled in a single interleaved schedule. Caveats: validation at 1.7% (not the 10⁻⁶ of the Dirichlet cases; adequate for O(0.1–1) relay signals, not precision surpluses); the trace-6 weight has an unresolved 2% discrepancy between the cycle-count and L-formula conventions (irrelevant here, g≈0 at that length); Maass list truncated at r₁₀. Open: Selberg surplus ledger, gap-decay vs the first eigenvalue r₁=9.53, and whether the parity-burn law has a geodesic analogue.

## 15. The Selberg ledger, itemized
